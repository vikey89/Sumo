Your Mac's processes, in a sumo arena.

案内

Your Activity Monitor, wearing a mawashi.

You don't need to know anything about sumo: the arena explains itself, and on first launch a five-step tour walks you through the real interface.

Every app is a wrestler

Weight is RAM, live every second. When an app asks for memory its wrestler eats a burger; when it frees some, it does the shiko, the ritual stomp.

横綱Chrome 大関Xcode 関脇Slack

A ranking that keeps moving

The banzuke orders apps by weight: the heaviest is the Yokozuna, then Ōzeki, Sekiwake, Komusubi. When the champion changes, confetti rains down.

Out of the ring, app quit

Drag a wrestler past the edge to quit the app. It always asks first, and system apps wear a padlock: they can't be touched.

立ち合いBouts and predictions

Who wins? Stake your chanko.

Every so often two apps face off in the ring. You have a few seconds before the tachiai, the opening charge, to tap your pick and place a bet.

  • Odds over their heads: the favourite pays little, the underdog up to ×8.
  • Slow motion at the clash, with a shaking camera.
  • Six real techniques (kimarite): yorikiri, oshidashi, uwatenage…
  • Coins fly into your balance when you call it right; three in a row earn a bonus.
力士Wrestlers with character

They sweat, they eat, they get angry.

These aren't bars in a chart: they're characters. Each one has its own face, build and belt colour.

  • Expressions: calm, happy when they win, sad when they fall, startled when you pick them up.
  • They sweat when CPU is high, and stuff themselves with burgers, pizza and soda as they grow.
  • They throw salt before every bout, just like real sumo.
  • Customise any app with a nickname and a mawashi colour.
押出Push out of the ring

Quitting an app has never felt this good.

Grab the wrestler, drag it past the straw bales and confirm. SUMO asks the app to quit politely, so it can still ask you to save.

  • Always confirmed: no app ever quits by accident.
  • The system is protected: macOS processes are a single fighter, the gyōji, the referee with the fan. It never fights and can't be touched.
  • Apps owned by other users, and SUMO itself, wear the padlock too.
開発Built for developers

No more five wrestlers called “node”.

Processes from node, python, java, ruby, bun, deno and other runtimes are named after what they're actually running, and the project they started from.

  • Recognises node_modules packages, python -m, .jar files, Java classes and scripts.
  • Different projects are different wrestlers: see at a glance which dev server is eating your RAM.
  • Only the tool and folder names leave the engine, never the command line.

本日Missions and recap

A reason to come back every day.

Missions challenge you to actually free up memory, and at the end of the day the recap tells you how things went in the ring.

  • Timed missions: “Let the Mac breathe”, “Put Android Studio on a diet”, “Expert eye”.
  • Trophies of the day: Heavyweight, Biggest eater, Best dieter, Bout champion.
  • A card to share: download an image of your Mac today, or copy it as text.
番付Ranks and details

Every app gets its own card.

Tap a wrestler: the camera follows it and its card opens with memory, CPU, today's peak, bouts won and the titles it has earned.

  • How many processes it is made of, and how much of your RAM it takes.
  • Today's wins and losses, and how long it has been in the arena.
  • From there you customise it or push it out of the ring.
用語Sumo guide

Learn sumo while you watch your RAM.

Banzuke, chanko, tachiai: the real terms stay, but each one has a tooltip and a glossary entry explaining what it means in sumo and what it means in the game.

  • Guided tour on first launch, with a first prediction to walk you through.
  • Glossary with kanji, meaning and role in the arena.
  • Shortcuts for everything: B bout, R recap, C fans, T basho, H arena only, ? guide.
熱Who turned the fans on?

The fans spin up. Now you know who did it.

The engine keeps the last ten minutes of CPU, even before you open the arena. Press C and every spike gets a name: which app, how long, and how much of the load was its fault.

  • Ten-minute timeline of your whole Mac's CPU, with the culprit written on every spike.
  • Down to the project: not “node”, but webpack · shop.
  • Click a spike and the arena points at the wrestler responsible.
場所The basho

A fifteen-day tournament, played by your apps.

Like real sumo, every basho lasts fifteen days. Each app's first bout of the day is its official one; the rest are exhibition. Press T for the standings.

  • Kachi-koshi at eight wins, make-koshi at eight losses, announced in the arena.
  • The hoshitori, the board of white and black stars, day by day.
  • After day fifteen the leader takes the yūshō and joins the past champions.
速報sumo top

A quick look, right in your terminal.

Don't feel like opening a tab? sumo top shows the banzuke live in your terminal: like top, but with sumo ranks.

  • Live: refreshes every 2 seconds and fits your window. q gives the terminal back as it was.
  • Sorted by memory, like the banzuke; press c to sort by CPU and m to go back.
  • Same names as the arena: you see vite · shop, not yet another node.
  • Works without the engine running, and prints a single snapshot when piped: sumo top | head.

The arena feels the pressure

Warm lights when the Mac is light; as RAM fills up, the arena turns red and starts to pulse.

Notifications when it matters

A macOS alert when an app goes past 8 GB or RAM passes 90%, naming the culprit. Never more than one in a while.

Arena only

Press H and only the ring and the crowd remain: perfect full screen on a second monitor.

Genuinely lightweight

30 frames per second when the arena is quiet, 60 during bouts. The engine uses about 2% CPU.

Gong and crowd

The bout gong and the cheering crowd, if you want them. The app asks you just once.

Respects “Reduce motion”

With the macOS accessibility setting on: no zoom, shakes, slow motion or particles.

安心

Play with your processes, not with your security.

An app that sees all your processes and can quit them has to be very careful. SUMO is.

Only on your Mac

The engine only listens on 127.0.0.1. No account, no external server: process data never leaves.

Other sites stay out

Every request is checked: no random web page can read your processes or quit apps, not even with DNS rebinding.

Quitting is your call

Every app you quit goes through a confirmation. The operating system, other users' apps and SUMO itself are protected.

No command lines

For developer processes only the tool and folder names come out: tokens and arguments stay where they are.
